How we're built

We start as framers

Plenty of companies can install trim, hang cabinets or lay a deck board. Far fewer can open a wall, work out what's actually carrying the load, and put it back correctly.

That matters more than it sounds. Almost every job on an older house turns up something behind the finish — wet sheathing, a notched joist, a header that was never really a header. A finish carpenter has to stop there and make phone calls. We keep going.

One crew · one contract · no second mobilisation

The surprises are the expensive part of construction. Being able to handle them in-house is the whole difference.

How we work

Same process, every job

01

We come out and look

Free, and thorough. We walk the whole property, not just the one thing you called about, and we probe anything that looks suspect rather than guessing from the driveway.

02

You get it in writing

A written assessment with photos and an honest urgency rating: what needs doing now, what can wait, what's worth keeping an eye on. It's yours to keep whether or not you hire us.

03

Then a real scope and price

Line-itemed, with materials named and warranty terms spelled out. If there's a chance we find something once we open it up, we tell you that up front and what it would cost.

04

We build it

Same crew start to finish. You get photos of anything we find behind the finish before we close it back up, and a walkthrough at the end before final payment.

What we find

How these jobs actually go

Rot & trim

Rotted fascia & rafter tails

Paint bubbling along the roof edge, soft wood behind it, and wet sheathing further in. Cut back to sound material, sheathing replaced, affected rafter tails sistered, and the run rebuilt in cellular PVC.

Rot & trim · Typically 3–6 days

Framing

Rear addition

A new wing on its own foundation, tied into an existing roofline. The difficult part is matching the pitch without creating a valley that traps water, which is why the tie-in gets detailed before anything is covered.

Additions · Typically 8–14 weeks

Renovations

Load-bearing wall removed

Opening a kitchen to the room behind it. An engineered beam set flush with the ceiling, with the load carried down through new posts and blocking to a footing rather than stopping at the beam.

Kitchens · Typically 6–12 weeks
