Home/Renovations

Renovations

Interiors, start to finish. Kitchens, bathrooms, basements and millwork — including the structural changes that make a floor plan work rather than just look better.

Our angle

We renovate structurally

Most renovation companies are finish operations. They're good at cabinets, tile and trim, and when the job needs a wall removed or a floor levelled, they bring somebody in.

We come at it the other way. We're framing carpenters who finish, which means the ambitious version of your floor plan — the one where the wall comes out and the ceiling opens up — is a normal job for us rather than a complication.

It also means fewer surprises. On a house built before 1980, opening a wall reveals something unexpected more often than not. Recognising it early is the difference between a conversation on Tuesday and a three-week delay.

The layout you actually want usually has a beam in it somewhere.

Living through it

How we run a job in an occupied house

  • Dust containment. Zip walls and negative air where we're demolishing. It's not perfect — nothing is — but it's the difference between one dusty room and a dusty house.
  • Floor and path protection on every route between the door and the work area.
  • A written schedule with the sequence and rough dates, updated when it changes, so you know when the trades are coming and when you'll be without a kitchen.
  • Consistent hours and a clean site at the end of each day.
  • One point of contact. You call one person about everything, including the subs.
  • Change orders in writing before the work happens, never after.

Renovations are disruptive. The goal isn't to pretend otherwise, it's to make the disruption predictable.
