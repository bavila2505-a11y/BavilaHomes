Rot & trim repair

Rotted fascia, soffit, corner boards and window casing — rebuilt in cellular PVC so it doesn't come back. And because we're framing carpenters, when the water reached the sheathing or the framing, we fix that too.

Assembly

Water doesn't stop at the trim board

Once water gets behind fascia, soffit or window casing, it keeps going — into the sheathing, the rim joist, the rafter tails, and sometimes the studs and sill plate.

Trim installers and siding crews have to stop when they find that. They call somebody else, or they close it back up and hope. We open it, show you how far it went, and put the structure back properly.

Condition

How to tell it's rot

Surface signs lag the actual damage by years. If you can see it from the ground, it's usually further along than it looks.

  • Paint bubbling, peeling or flaking on trim boards
  • Wood that feels soft or spongy, or takes a screwdriver with no resistance
  • Dark staining or streaking below gutters and rooflines
  • Gaps opening at corner boards or window casing
  • Soffit panels sagging, separating or dropping
  • Trim you've caulked and repainted more than once

If you've painted the same board twice, the wood under it is usually already gone.

Material

Rebuilt in PVC, so it's done

Cellular PVC doesn't absorb water, so it can't rot, and it doesn't need paint in order to survive. You paint it for the colour, not for protection.

Rot & insectsImmune. No moisture uptake, nothing for fungus or carpenter bees to feed on.
PaintOptional. Holds paint well when you want a colour, but a failed paint job doesn't put the board at risk.
MovementExpands and contracts more than wood with temperature, so it has to be fastened and gapped correctly. Done wrong you get buckling and open joints.
CostModerately more than clear cedar up front. Cheaper across any realistic lifespan once you stop repainting.
When we use woodHistoric profiles, custom milled trim, and anywhere a match matters more than maintenance. We'll tell you when that's the right call.
