Basements & interiors

Basement finishing that starts with water rather than drywall, plus interior trim, millwork and built-ins throughout the house.

First

Solve the water, then finish it

A finished basement is the most commonly regretted renovation we see, almost always for the same reason: it was finished before the moisture was dealt with.

Framing and drywall against a damp foundation wall creates exactly the conditions mould needs, hidden from view, and you find out about it in two years. We won't finish a basement we don't believe is dry, and we'll tell you at the assessment if we think that's the situation.

  • Find the source. Usually grading, gutters and downspouts discharging too close to the foundation. These are cheap fixes and they solve a surprising share of wet basements.
  • Then the foundation. Crack repair, interior drainage, sump, and a vapour barrier where it's warranted.
  • Then assess the structure. Sill plates, rim joists, girders and columns all live down here, and the basement is the one place you can actually see them before they get covered.
  • Then finish. Framed off the wall rather than against it, with appropriate insulation and materials that tolerate the occasional incident.

Every wet finished basement we tear out was dry the week it was built.

Constraints

What limits a basement

Ceiling heightThe binding constraint in most older New Jersey houses. Finished ceilings need a minimum height under code, and ducts, beams and pipes all eat into it. Sometimes solved by rerouting services; sometimes not solvable at all.
EgressAny basement bedroom needs a compliant means of escape — typically an egress window and window well, which means cutting the foundation. Plan for it early, it's not a small line item.
MoistureHas to be resolved first. Non-negotiable.
ServicesPanel, water heater, furnace and cleanouts all need to stay accessible, and code requires clearance around them. This shapes the layout more than people expect.
Bathroom drainageIf the drain line is above the basement floor, a basement bathroom needs an ejector pump. Doable, worth knowing the cost of up front.
